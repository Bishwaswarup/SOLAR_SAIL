{\small Technical note --- Bishwaswarup Nayak, Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru\\
\texttt{bishwaswarup@iisc.ac.in} \quad\textbullet\quad \today}
\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
\hrule
\vspace{6pt}

\noindent\textbf{Setting.} A face-on sail ($\alpha=0$) exerts a purely radial,
inverse-square force, so it is conservative and rescales solar gravity:
$(1-\mu)\to(1-\beta)(1-\mu)$. The problem is then a photogravitational CR3BP,
with pseudo-potential
$U=\tfrac12(x^2+y^2)+(1-\beta)(1-\mu)/r_1+\mu/r_2$.
On the segment between the primaries $r_1+r_2=1$ \emph{identically}, which is
what makes everything below exact rather than asymptotic.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{1. A closed form for the tidal-parity threshold.}
Write the smaller primary's direct contribution to the collinear
linearisation as the saddle strength $s=\mu/r_2^3$, and define tidal parity by
$s=1$, i.e.\ $r_2=\mu^{1/3}=3^{1/3}r_H$. At that radius the tidal acceleration
equals the standoff, $\mu/r_2^2=\mu^{1/3}=r_2$. Imposing this on the on-axis
balance $x=(1-\beta)(1-\mu)/r_1^2-\mu/r_2^2$, with $r_1=1-\mu^{1/3}$ exact, the
$\mu^{1/3}$ terms cancel and
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\beta_{\mathrm{crit}}=1-\bigl(1-\mu^{1/3}\bigr)^2=2\mu^{1/3}-\mu^{2/3}\;}
  \tag{1}
\end{equation}
for every mass ratio --- no expansion in $\mu$, no root-finding. Sun--Earth:
$\beta_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.028646456169$, standoff \SI{2158294}{\kilo\metre}.
Checked against independent Brent root-finding on $s(\beta)-1$ at machine
precision (\num{2.1e-16} at Sun--Earth; $\leq\num{1.9e-15}$ over a 61-point
sweep of $\mu\in[10^{-7},10^{-2}]$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{2. Why $s=1$ is not an arbitrary level.}
Generalise to $s=\kappa$, so $r_2=(\mu/\kappa)^{1/3}=(3/\kappa)^{1/3}r_H$. The
same substitution gives a closed form for \emph{every} $\kappa$,
\begin{equation}
  \beta(\kappa)=1-\frac{(1-r_2)^2}{1-\mu}\Bigl[(1-\mu)
  +\underbrace{\mu^{1/3}\kappa^{-1/3}(\kappa-1)}_{\textstyle\Delta(\kappa)}\Bigr],
  \tag{2}
\end{equation}
and the residue satisfies $\Delta(\kappa)=0 \iff \kappa=1$. Only at parity does
the smaller primary drop out of the balance identically, leaving
$1-\beta=r_1^2$ and hence the pure square (1). Two markers already in the
literature are members of this one family: the Hill-sphere exit
$r_2=r_H$ is exactly $\kappa=3$, and the classical $L_1$ sits at
$\kappa=3.0303$.

\noindent\textbf{3. A universal bound on the frequency ratio.}
The collinear parameter $A=(1-\beta)(1-\mu)/r_1^3+\mu/r_2^3$ is exactly
Richardson's $c_2$; a sail detunes it continuously. With
$\nu=\sqrt{c_2}$ out of plane and $\omega^2=\tfrac12[D-(c_2-2)]$,
$D=\sqrt{c_2(9c_2-8)}$, in plane, the ratio depends on $c_2$ alone. Setting
$\mathrm{d}(\nu^2/\omega^2)/\mathrm{d}c_2=0$ reduces to $D=2c_2$, hence
$9c_2^2-8c_2=4c_2^2$ and
\begin{equation}
  c_2^\star=\tfrac85,\qquad
  \lambda_u^2:\nu^2:\omega^2=7:8:9,\qquad
  \frac{\nu}{\omega}\Big|_{c_2^\star}=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3},
  \tag{3}
\end{equation}
so that over the whole admissible range $c_2>1$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\nu}{\omega}\in\Bigl[\tfrac{2\sqrt2}{3},\,1\Bigr),
  \qquad\text{a band \SI{5.72}{\percent} wide.}
  \tag{4}
\end{equation}
Both $c_2^\star$ and the bound are independent of $\mu$: they are properties of
the collinear linearisation, shared by every three-body system, sail or no sail.
Ceccaroni, Celletti \& Pucacco note that the centre-manifold frequencies are
``almost equal for all mass ratios''; (4) makes that exact and bounds the
detuning $\delta=\omega-\nu$ that normal-form treatments of the collinear
points rely on.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{4. No low-order commensurability is available.}
The largest rational below unity at denominator $q$ is $(q-1)/q$, so
$p{:}q$ can lie in (4) only if $(q-1)/q\geq2\sqrt2/3$, i.e.
$q\geq(1-2\sqrt2/3)^{-1}=17.485\ldots$, so $q\geq18$. \emph{The band contains
no commensurability of denominator below 18}; the lowest-order one it admits is
$17{:}18$, of order 35. Neither threshold is therefore a bifurcation --- $s>0$
for all finite $r_2$, so the saddle never disappears --- and I present both as
exact structural landmarks rather than dynamical transitions.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{5. Where this sits against flown hardware.}
Reducing $\beta$ from primary specifications ($\beta=\sigma^\ast/\sigma$,
$\sigma^\ast=\SI{1.5311}{\gram\per\metre\squared}$), no sail flown and deployed
reaches $\beta=0.0062$: LightSail-2 is $0.00613$, and the only \emph{measured}
value is IKAROS at $0.00062$. Tidal parity is a factor $4.7$ beyond the best
deployed sail and $1.4$ beyond Solar Cruiser as designed.

(iii) Whether the bound (4) is known; I have not found it stated.
